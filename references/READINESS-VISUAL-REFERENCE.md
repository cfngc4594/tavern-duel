# Readiness visual reference, corrected v9

Source: Blizzard BlizzCon 2013 press kit, Hearthstone-PriestVsHunter enUS.
https://blizzard.gamespress.com/BlizzCon-2013-Hearthstone-Press-Kit

Native source video: 1280 × 720, 30000/1001 fps. Inspected 04:49–04:52.5 (taunt) and 07:15–07:16.5 (ordinary minion), including multiple consecutive frames and pixel cross-sections.

The reference uses a frame-attached upper outline. The ordinary minion has an arch and sides; the taunt minion follows a shallow shield top, broad shoulders and near-vertical sides. Strong green fades near the number badges. The lower portrait edge and shield point are not surrounded by a closed green loop. Approximate native bright-band thickness is 4–6 px, with a 10–14 px total transition; typical core colors are warm lime, approximately RGB 90–120 / 248–251 / 60–80. These are video-space measurements, not recovered shader constants.

Across the inspected interval, geometry stays anchored. Local pale-bright patches vary, but neither a periodic expansion nor an orbiting highlight was established. The taunt subject also has separate yellow-white star effects, so those are not copied into generic readiness. v9 removes v8's invented large breathing, contour wobble, orbit and expanding Charge ring. The current green edge is deliberately stable; dynamic Zs and Charge sound remain.

The existing crown-shaped taunt plate did not match the reference silhouette, so its outer plate was minimally replaced with a shallow shield contour. Portraits, ordinary metallic oval frame, badges and gameplay remain as before. Generated art remains visibly different from Blizzard art; no pixel-exact reproduction is claimed.

Visibility uses the same legal readiness predicate as attacks, covering active turn, health, attack, freeze, animation, hidden/dead state, game/modals and board membership. Charge becomes ready after its staged summon; it retains its once-only original audio cue.
